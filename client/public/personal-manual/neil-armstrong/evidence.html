S02 · Johnson Space Center Oral History Project: Neil A. Armstrong

NASA Johnson Space Center · 2001-09-19

Armstrong describes self-directed aviation reading, research-pilot engineering, training/planning/testing, reliance on specialists, shared crew responsibility, risk thresholds, team reliability, and teaching.

Limit: Recorded three decades after Apollo 11; memory and self-presentation may shape emphasis.

S03 · Gemini’s First Docking Turns to Wild Ride in Orbit

NASA · 2016-03-16

After an uncontrolled roll, Armstrong isolated the stuck thruster, shut down the orbital system, used re-entry controls, stabilized the spacecraft, and accepted the mandatory abort.

Limit: Narrative summary; crew coordination detail is limited.

S04 · Apollo 11 Technical Crew Debriefing

NASA / Apollo 11 crew · 1969

The crew critiques simulator fidelity, favors operator checklists tied to use, calls for more realistic practice, and documents the transition to manual control over the lunar boulder field.

Limit: Technical focus reveals little about behavior outside mission work.

S05 · Apollo 11 Lunar Landing

Records program alarms, Mission Control go/no-go inputs, Armstrong’s manual-control check, the boulder field, Aldrin’s callouts, and the final landing sequence.

Limit: Radio brevity is task-constrained and should not be generalized to all communication.

S07 · Lunar Landing Research Vehicle: Technology Drivetrain of the Apollo Program

NASA · 2006

Shows that realistic free-flight training made human override usable when Apollo 11’s automated landing path led toward unsafe terrain.

Limit: Program-level history; not a complete account of Armstrong’s individual contribution.
